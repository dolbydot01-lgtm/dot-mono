MARKETS AND TECHNOLOGY

Wall Street suffers a sharp sell-off as AI worries mount

Wall Street fell sharply on Monday as fallout from Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia chief executive soured enthusiasm for artificial intelligence stocks. That raised the stakes for Nvidia’s earnings report today and for Anthropic’s initial public offering later this week. The selling gathered pace through the session, with the S&P 500 and the Dow both down well over 1%, while the Nasdaq suffered the steepest percentage loss.

The chip index dragged the Nasdaq more than 10% below its record close of 6 October, which confirms the index is in a correction. In mid-June the semiconductor index was up 106.6% for the year. Its 2026 gain has narrowed to about 43%, which is still roughly four times the gain in the broader S&P 500 so far this year.

Nvidia fell 4.2%, the worst of the “Magnificent Seven” group of megacap AI-linked companies. Intel and Micron each dropped more than 6%. The AI cloud providers CoreWeave and Nebius lost 6.2% and 7.5%, and server makers Dell and Super Micro Computer fell 6.6% and 5.8%. Alphabet, Amazon and Meta each slid between 2.5% and 3.3%.

Breadth was poor. On the New York Stock Exchange, falling shares outnumbered rising ones by more than three to one, with 56 new 52-week highs against 493 new lows. On the Nasdaq, 1,129 shares rose and 3,613 fell.

Ross Mayfield, an investment strategist at Baird, described a market swinging day to day between AI names surging and then falling hard, alongside a rotation into other sectors. He said investors did not look to be panic-selling and abandoning the asset class altogether, but probing other corners of the equity market.

Nvidia reports earnings with no permanent chief executive

Nvidia reports its quarterly results today after the Wall Street close, giving investors a fresh look at the outlook for the AI chip giant. Its value has fallen sharply since Jensen Huang’s sudden resignation last week. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is presenting the results in an interim leadership role.

Analysts expect Nvidia to report earnings up 90.4% from a year earlier, on revenue of $109.10 billion. Investors are expected to focus on demand for advanced AI processors, revenue growth and margins, and on the company’s strategy following the management change.

Reuters reports that Huang resigned after allegations of an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. In South Korea’s SBS News, Donald Trump is reported as saying it is uncertain whether Alice Huang, who had the affair with Jensen Huang, was really a Chinese spy, and that she might not be. Nvidia’s board is reportedly not considering bringing Huang back.

His departure also had policy consequences. The Trump administration issued a new rule restricting China’s access to the best AI chips from companies like Nvidia, ahead of Trump’s expected meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping. Separate reports said the US Commerce Department is moving to restrict arrangements that let Chinese companies use Nvidia computing capacity in facilities outside China. That would add to existing controls on advanced chip exports and could affect demand for AI computing infrastructure.

The unease spread to Asia this morning. South Korea’s Kospi opened 1.8% lower, tracking Wall Street’s losses and worries over AI spending. Investors are also watching Anthropic’s planned Nasdaq debut on Thursday, which is seen as a test of appetite for AI companies after the recent correction. Bloomberg Businessweek has noted that Wall Street is increasingly concerned that AI is not yet producing enough revenue or profit to justify the huge spending on infrastructure.

London’s FTSE 100 slips as miners and tech shares fall

London’s FTSE 100 closed lower on Monday, down 21.01 points, or 0.2%, at 10,881.55. It had reached 10,939.35 at midday, but gains reversed as US technology stocks slid. The fall ended the index’s recent upward run, which had taken it to a three-month closing high on Friday. The FTSE 250 fell 0.4% to 24,700.06 and the AIM All-Share lost 0.6% to 752.34. In Europe, the CAC 40 fell 0.2% and the DAX 0.6%.

Mining shares were among the biggest casualties after disappointing Chinese economic data and weaker industrial metal prices. Anglo American fell 4.3% to 3,605p, Antofagasta 3.1% to 3,049p and Fresnillo 2.5%. Technology investment trusts also suffered: Polar Capital Technology Trust fell 2% and Scottish Mortgage 1.7%.

There were some bright spots. Coca-Cola HBC rose 2.6% to 4,350p after saying it will begin trading on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange’s Main Board from Monday 23 November, while keeping its London and Athens listings. Croda gained 1.6% to 3,822p after Deutsche Bank upgraded it from hold to buy, citing potential double-digit earnings growth and possible buybacks. On the FTSE 250, Ceres Power rose 2.3% after announcing a manufacturing licence deal for its solid oxide fuel cell technology with a privately owned European energy technology company. Earlier in the day, defence stocks led the index, with BAE Systems and Babcock both up 2.2% after a Morningstar report said recent volatility had made valuations in the sector more attractive. Legal & General fell 2.2% at midday as pension risk transfer volumes disappointed.

INTEREST RATES

Rate rises in focus on both sides of the Atlantic

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh said on Monday that technology will have a significant impact on the economy. He said the possible outcomes range from rising growth with broadly shared gains to higher unemployment and a potential slowdown if the expected returns on AI investment disappoint. Minutes from the Fed’s latest policy meeting are due on Wednesday and could offer clues ahead of next month’s decision. According to CME FedWatch, markets price a 93.7% likelihood of a 25 basis point rate rise in December. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.14% and the 30-year to 5.48%.

In the UK, employment figures are due today and consumer price inflation on Wednesday. Both could shape expectations for the Bank of England. Goldman Sachs expects the unemployment rate to ease to 4.6% from 4.7%, and private-sector regular pay growth to rise to 3.1%. It expects annual inflation to rise to 3.7% in October from 3.5% in September, with core inflation rising to 2.9%.

The Bank’s chief economist, Huw Pill, has indicated that further rate rises may be necessary. He said that markets expecting Bank Rate to approach 5% next year is not unreasonable. Sterling weakened to $1.3189 against the dollar on Monday as investors weighed the prospect of more increases. Higher borrowing costs remain a concern for businesses and households while growth faces pressure from inflation.

In the euro area, industrial production recovered by 0.2% in September, though output was still 1.5% lower than a year earlier. The European Central Bank warned that a sharp reassessment of AI valuations could threaten financial stability, given the scale of investment and borrowing behind the sector. Also due today are US retail sales and industrial production figures and the Reserve Bank of Australia’s meeting minutes.

Donald Trump said he could meet North Korean leader Kim Jong-un in China on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Trump also commented on Alice Huang, who had an affair with former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. He said it remained uncertain whether she was a Chinese spy and that she might not be. Nvidia’s board is reportedly not considering Huang’s return, even though it has been raised.

Elsewhere in the region, South Korean President Lee Jae-myung is attending the ASEAN summit in the Philippines. Today he is holding a summit with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, six months after their last meeting.

The chip rule is already moving markets. Reuters reports that it followed Huang’s departure and covers the best AI chips from companies like Nvidia. On Monday the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 4.6% and Nvidia lost 4.2%, so any softening or hardening of US–China tech policy at the summit would matter well beyond the diplomatic agenda. Nvidia reports earnings later today.

What to watch: because Trump has tied a Kim meeting to Xi, there may be no confirmation until close to the summit itself. Any Trump–Xi talks will also be read against Monday’s chip restrictions and the market turmoil in AI stocks.

South Korea and China tighten exam security against AI cheating

South Korea’s college entrance exam, the CSAT, is two days away, and anxiety about cheating with artificial intelligence is growing. Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin has acknowledged that blocking AI cheating completely is impossible. The government has announced that all smart devices, including phones, will be collected after being switched off at test sites, and that glasses and watches will be inspected closely. Measures to block Wi-Fi were announced yesterday, and more steps are due today.

The concern has been sharpened by a case this morning. A man who topped the certified tax accountant exam admitted cheating with an ultra-small camera and AI, and appeared before police. He apologised to other test-takers and said he would take responsibility for his actions.

A report from Beijing shows how China approaches the problem for the Gaokao, which had 12.9 million test-takers in June. The rules ban any device that can send, receive or record information. Test-takers pass through a security gate that detects metal, then are checked again with handheld detectors at the classroom door. If an alarm sounds, the student must remove their glasses for further checks. Ordinary glasses are allowed, but glasses with AI functions are not.

Inside the exam rooms, AI analyses CCTV footage from every classroom in real time. It alerts a central control room to behaviour such as repeated head-turning, hands hidden under the desk, working before the start signal or continuing after the end signal. Major AI services also restricted features during the exam: Alibaba’s Qwen and DeepSeek suspended image recognition that solves photographed questions. The report notes that China’s reliance on domestic AI services makes this control easier.

Sources: SBS News, Seoul and Beijing

South Korea’s Supreme Court to rule on ex-PM Han Duck-soo

South Korea’s Supreme Court will rule on Thursday on the insurrection case against former Prime Minister Han Duck-soo, one day before his current detention period expires. The court will also rule that day on three major suspicions involving former first lady Kim Keon-hee, including the Deutsch Motors stock manipulation case.

Han’s case concerns former President Yoon Suk Yeol’s declaration of martial law in December 2024. In January, the Seoul Central District Court sentenced him to 23 years, making him the first former cabinet minister to receive a lower-court ruling on charges directly tied to martial law. An appeals court cut the sentence to 15 years in May but still found he had played a key role in the insurrection. His lawyers took the case to the Supreme Court, which sent his appeal and that of former interior minister Lee Sang-min to its Grand Bench for a joint hearing. It has already extended Han’s detention three times.

The timing has become political. On 10 November the Democratic Party said the special prosecutor law requires the Supreme Court to rule within three months of the appeals sentencing, and called the delay a clear violation. It warned that releasing Han would amount to the court freeing him. Lee Sang-min’s detention expires on 18 December.

Kim Keon-hee has been detained since August 2025. In April an appeals court raised her sentence to four years, up from 20 months, for stock manipulation and bribery, reversing an earlier acquittal in the Deutsch Motors case. Her lawyers appealed. In late October the Supreme Court issued an additional two-month detention warrant over concerns that she could flee or destroy evidence.

UNITED STATES

Nationwide Emergency Alert System test

FEMA and the Federal Communications Commission are running a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System today at about 2:20 p.m. Eastern, which is 1:20 p.m. Central and 7:20 p.m. in the UK. Radio and television broadcasters, cable systems, satellite radio and television providers, and wireline video providers across the country will interrupt normal programming to relay a test message for roughly one minute. Regular programming then resumes.

The message states that it is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System issued by FEMA, covering the United States for a window from 2:20 to 2:50 p.m. Eastern. It says this is only a test and that no action is required by the public. The test uses the standard alert tones, which support people who are deaf or hard of hearing, and some broadcasters may also air the message in Spanish.

FEMA and the FCC announced the date in advance, on 5 November, so the public would know what to expect. They are coordinating with communications providers and with federal, state, tribal and territorial agencies to carry out the test.

The test does not involve Wireless Emergency Alerts, the messages sent to phones. That system was tested nationally in 2023, and the last test of the broadcast Emergency Alert System before today was on 4 October 2023.

The system gives the president a way to address the country or share life-saving information during a national emergency. FEMA is required by law to test it at least once every three years, so that technical and operational problems can be found. If a real emergency or significant weather occurs today, the test could be postponed. The backup date is Thursday 3 December.
